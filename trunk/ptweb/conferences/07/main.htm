Seventh Biennial Ptolemy Miniconference

Tuesday, February 13, 2007 - Berkeley, California

The Seventh Biennial Ptolemy Miniconference will be held on Tuesday, February 13, 2007 in 306 Soda Hall on the UC Berkeley Campus. The Ptolemy project studies modeling, simulation, and design of concurrent, real-time, embedded systems. The focus is on assembly of concurrent components.

The Ptolemy Miniconference is an opportunity for research collaborators and Ptolemy users and extenders from industry, academia, and government to get together, present their work to the Ptolemy community, and hear about related research and results. It is typically held every two years.

This year, we have asked the Kepler community to give presentations and posters. Kepler is a cross-project collaboration to develop open source tools for Scientific Workflows and is currently based on the Ptolemy II system for heterogeneous concurrent modeling and design. Thus, we have asked Matthew Jones from UC Santa Barbara, one of the initiators of the Kepler Project to co-chair this conference with Professor Edward A. Lee.

Details about the conference will be announced on the Ptolemy-hackers mailing list and on this web page.

We will offer a Ptolemy Developer's Tutorial on Monday, February 12, 2007 in 540 A/B Cory on the UC Berkeley campus. This tutorial is for Java programmers who are interested in extending Ptolemy.

The Center for Hybrid and Embedded Software Systems (CHESS) will hold a meeting on the following day, Wednesday, February 14, 2007.

The 2007 Berkeley EECS Annual Research Symposium (BEARS) will be on Thursday, February 15.

November 10, 2006: The conference program has be determined.
